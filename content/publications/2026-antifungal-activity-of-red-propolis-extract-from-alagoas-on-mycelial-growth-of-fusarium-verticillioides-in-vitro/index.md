abstract: "Maize (Zea mays) is a staple food worldwide. Fusarium verticillioides, an endophytic and opportunistic pathogen prevalent in the tropics, causes ear and stalk rot, rotten kernels and seedling damage, and produces fumonisins, hepatotoxic mycotoxins regulated by ANVISA (limit: 2 µg/g) and detected in more than 90% of Brazilian maize samples. This study evaluated the in vitro effect of Alagoas red propolis extract, rich in phenolics and flavonoids, on F. verticillioides mycelial growth and estimated its IC50 in two independent assays. The extract inhibited F. verticillioides at every concentration tested, in a dose-dependent way (relative IC50 = 3.99–5.74 ppm; R² = 0.93–0.95; 50% inhibition versus control between 6.25 and 12.5 ppm), reaching a plateau of about 78% inhibition from 75 ppm (colony diameter 73.6 mm in the control versus 16.1 mm at 150 ppm), indicating a fungistatic effect. Results were reproducible in both assays. Alagoas red propolis is a promising biocompatible alternative to control Fusarium ear rot and mitigate fumonisin risk in maize."
