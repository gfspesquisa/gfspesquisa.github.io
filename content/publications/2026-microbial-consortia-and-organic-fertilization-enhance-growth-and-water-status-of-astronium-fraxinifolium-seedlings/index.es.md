abstract: "Combining organic substrates with microbial inoculants represents a promising approach to optimize forest seedling production. This study evaluated the effects of composted sewage sludge (CSS) mixed with sugarcane bagasse (1:1, v:v) and microbial consortia on fungal root colonization, water use efficiency (WUE), and initial development of Astronium fraxinifolium seedlings. The experiment used 6 L pots in a randomized block design with a 2×4 factorial scheme: CSS (with/without; 23 g per pot) and four inoculation treatments — Control: without inoculation, Bacillus subtilis (BS; strain CCTB04; 2×10⁸ CFU mL⁻¹), Rhizophagus clarus (RhC; 75 spores/100 g soil), and BS+RhC (n=5). After 150 days, shoot dry mass, root dry mass, collar diameter and height were measured to calculate the Dickson Quality Index (DQI). WUE was determined as the ratio of net photosynthesis to transpiration. Root colonization was assessed in 100 fine root segments per sample and spore density in 100 g of dry soil. Data were analyzed by Shapiro-Wilk normality test, ANOVA, and Tukey test (p≤0.05). CSS+BS and CSS+RhC yielded the highest DQI values. Both BS inoculation and CSS improved WUE. BS and RhC increased arbuscular root colonization and soil spore density, reflecting superior seedling quality. The combined application of CSS and microbial consortia provides a sustainable biotechnological strategy to produce high-quality A. fraxinifolium seedlings."
