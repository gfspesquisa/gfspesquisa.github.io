abstract: "Fusarium verticillioides, an endophytic and opportunistic pathogen prevalent in the tropics, causes ear and stalk rot and seedling damage in maize and produces fumonisins, hepatotoxic mycotoxins regulated by ANVISA (limit: 2 µg/g) and detected in more than 90% of Brazilian maize samples. This study evaluated the antifungal effect of silver ions (Ag+) carried in dolomite on the in vitro mycelial growth of F. verticillioides and estimated its EC50. Pure dolomite (PD) or dolomite + AgNO3 (DSN) was added to PDA (10.5–700 ppm Ag+), plates were inoculated with 5-mm mycelial discs (n = 5) and colony diameter was measured every 24 h, with analysis at 120 h (ANOVA, Tukey HSD, α = 0.05; nonlinear regression for EC50). Silver-doped dolomite inhibited F. verticillioides in a dose-dependent way from 175 ppm Ag+ (EC50 ≈ 258 ppm, 95% CI 228–292; 78–80% inhibition at 525–700 ppm), with no effect at ≤ 87.5 ppm. Pure dolomite increased growth by about 26%, so the antifungal effect comes from Ag+. Results confirm the fungistatic potential of silver ions and support in vivo trials in Zea mays."
